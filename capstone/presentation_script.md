# Presentation script (about 5 minutes)

**How to record:** open `Executive_Churn_Briefing.pdf` full screen, start any screen recorder (Zoom, Loom, QuickTime, Meet), read the lines below slide by slide, then upload the video to the same folder (or a view-only link) and add it to the README.

**Slide 1: Title (10 sec)**
"This briefing answers two questions for a non-technical decision-maker: who is likely to leave next, and is it worth acting? Everything uses synthetic data, so it demonstrates the method rather than real rates."

**Slide 2: Bottom line (50 sec)**
"About one in four customers churned, costing us roughly sixty-nine thousand dollars of monthly revenue, 18 percent of the total. Our score finds a small High-risk group of 476 customers that churns at 70 percent. My recommendation is a one-month pilot on that group with a holdout. The expected result ranges from a loss of about eight thousand to a gain of about twenty-six thousand dollars in year one, so I am asking you to approve a pilot, not a full rollout. Outreach breaks even only if we save around 8 percent of those at-risk customers."

**Slide 3: Insights 1 and 2 (50 sec)**
"First, risk is concentrated. The High tier is ten percent of customers, and the top twenty percent by score contains 46 percent of everyone who leaves. Second, contract type. Monthly customers churn at 32 percent versus 11 percent on annual plans. That suggests an annual-plan offer is a cheap lever, but this is an association. We have not shown that switching someone prevents churn."

**Slide 4: Insights 3 and 4 (50 sec)**
"Third, usage decline is an early warning. 907 customers cut logins by 30 percent or more and churn at 41 percent, double steady users. Fourth, low feature adoption. 1,274 customers use two or fewer features and churn at 46 percent. That is our largest group we can act on, mainly through onboarding."

**Slide 5: Insight 5, the economics (60 sec)**
"The fifth insight is the one that keeps us honest. The customers most likely to leave pay only about fifty-six dollars a month, so each save is worth little. With an assumed forty dollars per contact and twelve months of value per save, the base case nets about three and a half thousand dollars, the low case loses almost eight thousand, and the high case gains twenty-six thousand. None of these save rates has been measured. They are planning assumptions, which is exactly why I recommend a pilot. The dashboard link has sliders so you can test your own assumptions."

**Slide 6: Recommended action (40 sec)**
"Match the play to the reason customers leave: personal outreach for disengaging customers, a named owner for ticket and billing problems, structured onboarding for new accounts, a cheap annual offer for stable monthly customers, and no discounts for the committed core. The pilot runs one month with half the group held out. We go ahead if the measured save rate is above about 8 percent, and stop or redesign if it is below."

**Slide 7: Limitations (40 sec)**
"Please keep these in mind. The data is synthetic. Relationships are associations, not proven causes. The score ranks customers but is not a probability. The impact figures rest on assumptions, so read the range rather than the base case. The thresholds were set by hand and need review on real outcomes. That is why the next step is a controlled pilot. Thank you."
